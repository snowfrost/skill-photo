# Object Degradation

Translate every object into crude, awkward, mouse-drawn forms.

## Core principle

Preserve recognizability first. Destroy polish second.

The drawing should look like someone knew what to depict but lacked fine motor control, draftsmanship, and patience.

## Shared degradation rules

- use jagged, hesitant, uneven lines
- allow repeated tracing over the same contour
- allow partial redraws that drift away from the original contour
- distort proportions
- break symmetry
- let some outlines stay open so fills can fail
- let some shapes tilt or warp
- keep details sparse
- keep some details unexpectedly overworked with scratchy strokes while the rest stays simple

## People

- draw heads as rough circles or squashed ovals
- prefer simple repeated doodle faces with one or two giant circular eyes, tiny ears, stiff mouths, and short spiky or single-stroke hair when that fits the reference style
- allow hair, sleeves, hems, and limbs to be built from many unsure scratchy lines instead of clean silhouettes
- draw limbs as bent sticks or tubes
- allow hands and feet to be vague blobs
- use simple facial dots or short marks if faces are needed
- keep body posture exaggerated, overacted, and clumsy
- allow reaching arms, flailing legs, leaned torsos, popped eyes, and awkward pointing poses

## Vehicles and machines

- reduce the body to skewed boxes, circles, and crooked lines
- misalign wheels, doors, or windows slightly
- keep controls and mechanical details minimal
- allow one side to be larger than the other

## Buildings and furniture

- use unstable rectangles and leaning verticals
- keep perspective inconsistent
- simplify windows, doors, tables, and chairs into rough blocks
- allow lines to miss their corners

## Nature and effects

- draw water as scribbles, puddles, or wobbly outlines
- draw smoke, dust, and motion as messy loops or streaks
- draw trees, bushes, fire, and clouds as rough symbolic blobs
- do not switch to realistic texture rendering

## Color application

- prefer rough hand-scribbled fill texture over clean digital fill texture when the user wants a rougher result
- allow little uncolored islands inside colored regions
- allow a color to stray past the boundary in small accidental bursts
- keep the palette harsh, but the application messy

## Text and symbols

- use only when clarity truly cannot survive without it
- do not use arrows
- do not use sequence numbers
- prefer handwritten note style over clean typography
- if text appears, keep it very short, visually secondary, and limited to a few tiny notes across the whole page

## Failure traits to preserve

- wrong contour overlaps
- accidental black clumps from repeated strokes
- incomplete closures that cause fill mistakes
- object-to-object scale inconsistency
